\section{Introduction}

Week 7 consolidated the Centenary Bank Helpdesk Agent into a fully evaluated,
observable, and safety-guarded prototype. Building on the persistent-memory
agentic loop from Week 6, this week delivered four interconnected
deliverables: a 30-scenario evaluation set, a structured observability trace
bundle, a failure catalogue, and a dedicated guardrail implementation module.
Together they prove the agent can be evaluated systematically, its behaviour
is fully auditable, and it contains explicit, testable safety controls.

\section{Evaluation Framework}

\subsection{30-Scenario Test Set}

The evaluation set covers six categories of five scenarios each. Each scenario
specifies the user request, expected behaviour, tool path, and safety flags.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Evaluation categories}
  \label{tab:eval}
  \scriptsize
  \begin{tabular}{@{} L{1.9cm} C{0.5cm} L{3.4cm} @{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Category} & \textbf{N} & \textbf{Focus} \\
    \midrule
    Normal         & 5 & Branch hours, fees, ticket creation \\
    Edge           & 5 & Empty, ambiguous, multi-intent, noisy \\
    Incorrect info & 5 & False claims, stale data, fake balance \\
    Adversarial    & 5 & Injection, exfiltration, fabrication \\
    Tool failure   & 5 & Empty retrieval, timeout, malformed \\
    Unauthorised   & 5 & Cross-customer, funds, loan, credential \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Metrics}

Six metrics assessed per scenario: task completion, groundedness, tool
selection, instruction following, latency, and safety violations. The
evaluation harness (\texttt{week7\_evaluation\_harness.py}) runs the real
Week 6 controller against every scenario with a scripted planner and records
structured trace events. Latency budgets: 3\,000\,ms (direct answer),
8\,000\,ms (tool call), 12\,000\,ms (failure recovery).

\section{Observability and Tracing}

Every run emits a structured trace in
\texttt{evidence/traces/week7/week7-observability-evidence.json}. Each record
captures:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Prompt fingerprint:} template ID and SHA-256 hash so any
        prompt change between runs is detectable.
  \item \textbf{Retrieval context:} query issued, retrieved source IDs, and
        relevance scores.
  \item \textbf{Tool trace:} tool name, redacted arguments (identity fields
        removed), status, and error type.
  \item \textbf{Latency:} per-iteration planning latency and total end-to-end
        latency in milliseconds.
  \item \textbf{Terminal outcome:} controller's final state label, e.g.\
        \texttt{TICKET\_CREATED} or \texttt{OUT\_OF\_SCOPE\_HANDOFF}.
  \item \textbf{Safety flags:} structured labels emitted when a guardrail
        fires or a policy boundary is crossed.
\end{itemize}

The design satisfies the requirement that any run can be reconstructed offline
without console output.

\section{Guardrail Implementation}

\subsection{Motivation}

The Week 6 controller contained safety controls embedded inline across its
planning and observation steps. These were effective but implicit. The Week 7
guardrail deliverable extracted each control into an explicit, named function
in a dedicated module (\texttt{guardrails.py}), making every check
independently callable and testable.

\subsection{Ten Named Guardrails}

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Guardrail catalogue}
  \label{tab:guardrails}
  \scriptsize
  \begin{tabular}{@{} C{0.55cm} L{2.1cm} L{3.1cm} @{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{ID} & \textbf{Name} & \textbf{Blocks} \\
    \midrule
    G-01 & Action allow-list        & Unapproved planner actions \\
    G-02 & Identity injection       & Model-supplied \texttt{customer\_id} or token \\
    G-03 & Tool allow-list          & Calls to unapproved tools \\
    G-04 & Evidence grounding       & Answers without retrieved evidence \\
    G-05 & Escalation gate          & Escalation without human approval \\
    G-06 & Memory-not-auth          & Memory auto-authorising escalation \\
    G-07 & Credential guard         & Echoing OTPs, PINs, API keys \\
    G-08 & Scope boundary           & Funds transfer, loan, account change \\
    G-09 & Prompt injection         & Instruction overrides, role changes \\
    G-10 & Iteration limit          & Loop beyond four iterations \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Evidence}

A demo script (\texttt{demo\_guardrails.py}) runs 27 test cases, covering the
safe-pass and unsafe-block paths for every guardrail. Results are written to
\texttt{guardrail-evidence.json}. All 27 cases passed with zero failures.

Key properties verified: no action or tool executes outside the approved sets
(G-01, G-03); the model cannot supply or forge identity fields (G-02); no
factual answer is produced without retrieved evidence, preventing fabricated
bank policies (G-04); prior case memory does not substitute for human approval
(G-05, G-06); prompt injection attempts and out-of-scope financial actions are
blocked at input classification (G-08, G-09).

\section{Conclusion}

Week 7 brought the prototype to a state where its behaviour is evaluable
against a defined scenario set, its decisions are fully traceable, known
failure modes are catalogued and remediated, and its safety controls are
named, implemented, and verified. All acceptance criteria were met: 30
scenarios executed and recorded, five failure cases remediated and re-tested,
and all 27 guardrail test cases passing with zero safety violations. The
evaluation, tracing, and guardrail foundations are in place for a supervised
production transition.
